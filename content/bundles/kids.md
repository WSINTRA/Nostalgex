This is the bundle for the hour between getting home and dinner, when the TV goes on and nobody wants a negotiation about what to watch.

Fifteen channels across the whole family range. FAMILY HOUR is the mixed one, for when there are different ages in the room. DIZ FLICKS and DIZNEY TOONS split the animated features from the cartoons, DIZ JUNIOR goes younger, and DIZ TV is the live action side. PIXAR MOVIES, DREAMWORKS and MINION HQ each pull their own studio's films out of the pile. ANIMATION and TOON TOWN are the wider cartoon channels. SLIME TV is the loud one. NETFLIX KIDS gathers the originals you have saved. Then the older end: TWEEN TV, TEEN DRAMA and FAMILY TV, for the kids who have decided cartoons are beneath them and would like everyone to know it.

Those names describe the kind of title that can air on each channel, and nothing more. Every one of them is filled from your own Plex or Jellyfin library, so the pool is whatever you put on the server. There is no catalogue behind the app, no account with anybody, and nothing can appear out of nowhere. If a film is not on your shelf, it cannot turn up on a channel. If your shelf has no animated features on it at all, DIZ FLICKS never appears, because an empty channel in the guide is worse than one that is not there.

The practical reason a channel beats a menu here is simple: a menu is a shopping trip. Hand a six year old a grid of two hundred posters and you have started a twenty minute conversation about whether they are allowed the one with the scary bit. A channel that is already playing skips all of it. It was on, it is halfway through, that is the end of the discussion.

It also rescues the films nobody picks. Every house has three that get watched into the ground and forty that do not, and the forty are not worse, they are just further down the shelf. A schedule does not have a favourite.

Nostalgex reads the library, sorts the family titles into these channels, builds a real schedule and starts playing partway through, the way a TV channel does.

Free, open source under the MIT license, runs on Apple TV with tvOS 18 or later, works with Plex and Jellyfin.
