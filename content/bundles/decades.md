Decades is the bluntest bundle in the app and that is the appeal.

Seven channels: THE 60S, THE 70S, THE 80S, THE 90S, THE 00S, THE 10S, THE 20S. No genre, no mood, no clever grouping. One channel per decade, sorted by the year on the film, playing whatever you own from that stretch of time.

It is the bundle people use when they know exactly what they are in the mood for and it is not a genre. Some nights you do not want a comedy or a thriller, you want 1987. The lighting, the cars, the synth over the opening titles, the fact that everyone is wearing too much denim. A decade channel gives you that in a way a genre channel never does, because it mixes the action movie and the teen comedy and the made for TV thing you forgot existed, which is what actual TV did.

It is also the fastest way to find out what your library really is. Turn this bundle on and the shape of your collection shows up in the guide: four channels running all day and three that never appear. Most people are heavy in the 90s and 2000s and almost empty before 1970. That is useful to see.

The mechanics are the same as the rest of the app. Nostalgex reads the Plex or Jellyfin library you already have, sorts titles by release year into these seven channels, builds a real schedule and starts playing mid film. Nothing ships with the app and nothing streams from outside your own server. A decade with nothing in it does not get a channel.

One pairing worth trying: leave THE 80S or THE 90S on next to the retro bundle. The retro channels pick the nostalgic stuff, the warm end of the decade. The decade channel plays everything from those years, including the films nobody got sentimental about, and that is where you find the thing you have not seen.

Free, open source under the MIT license, Apple TV on tvOS 18 or later.
